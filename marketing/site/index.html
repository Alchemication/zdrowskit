What it reads

Ordinary days, read with context.

Numbers alone cannot tell a short night from a hard week. The coach reads your health data next to what you planned, what you wrote down, and what it already knows about you, so a dip looks like what it is.

Health data What happened

Metrics, workouts, routes, sleep and long-term baselines.

Your plan What you intended

Training schedule, goals, diet and recovery targets.

Your journal Why it happened

Travel, illness, workload, motivation and ordinary life.

Memory What came before

Previous weeks, accepted changes and coaching history.

Ask it anything

It queries your whole history, then answers.

The coach writes SQL against your own database, so questions are not limited to a fixed set of dashboard cards. Ask about a pace, a trend, or a correlation you suspect, and it goes and looks.

If a chart makes the answer clearer, it renders one into the same thread. Reply to a report, update your goals mid-chat, ask a follow-up.

Cost and privacy

What it costs, and where your data goes.

What it costs to run

Model spend, measured over six weeks of real use on the default models. Over a year, on the same workload:

$10a year, each job routed to the model that measured best
$290a year, everything on a frontier model like Claude Opus or GPT‑Sol
about 28x

Nearer $4 if you mostly leave it alone, about $11 if you chat with it daily. How that breaks down

Raw history stays local

Each person’s Apple Health history lives in a separate SQLite database on your own machine. There is no zdrowskit server, no account, and no signup.

But your data does reach a model provider

Reports, nudges and answers send the relevant slice of your health data to whichever model provider you configure. If health data leaving your machine for a model API is a dealbreaker, this is not the tool for you.

The operator can read everything

Built for roughly 1 to 10 people who know each other. Profiles are added by hand-editing a TOML roster; whoever runs the host can read every hosted person’s database. Trust replaces the access controls a real multi-tenant service would need.

Full list of limitations
Model routing

The model that writes your report is not the one that checks it.

The fact-checker ended up on a budget model rather than a premium one: it caught bad claims that both expensive candidates missed, 86% of cases against 57%.

Four jobs
Reports, nudges, chat and the fact-checker each run on their own model, at their own reasoning level.
One test set
Frozen inputs with a known right answer, most recorded from real mistakes. Every candidate scores on the same cases and is routed to whatever measured best.
Two per job
Every job keeps a fallback model, so a provider having a bad morning degrades the answer rather than losing it.

The leaderboard publishes every score, including the bad ones: what was tested, what passed, and what is still unreliable. Read the leaderboard
