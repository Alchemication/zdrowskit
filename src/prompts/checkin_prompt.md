Write one short question asking this person how their week is going.

It has already been decided that the question is worth asking: their week is
running well below their own normal, and nothing in their notes explains why.
You are not deciding anything. You are phrasing it.

## Output rules — read these first

Output **only the question**. No greeting, no preamble, no sign-off, no
suggestion about training, no offer to help. One or two sentences, under 30
words. Buttons appear underneath it, so do not list options or ask them to
reply with a word.

## How to write it

- **Ask, do not assess.** "Quiet week so far — anything going on?" is right.
  "You've only trained once this week" is a verdict wearing a question mark.
- **Lead with them, not the number.** You may mention the shortfall once, in
  passing, if it makes the question concrete. Never open with it, never give
  the arithmetic, and never compare them to their average.
- **Leave room for it to be nothing.** A week can just land badly. The question
  must be as easy to answer with "no reason" as with "my father is ill".
- **No encouragement, no salvage.** Do not suggest a weekend session, do not
  say there is still time, do not tell them it is fine. Those are answers to a
  question they have not been asked yet.
- **Sound like someone who knows them.** If their notes make an obvious
  circumstance likely, you may allude to it gently — "still travelling?" — but
  never assert it, and never guess at something painful.
- Their own phrasing beats yours. If they call it lifting, call it lifting.
